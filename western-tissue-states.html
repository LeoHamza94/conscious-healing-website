The Western Physiomedical States model, popularized and refined by contemporary medical herbalist Matthew Wood, represents the foundational physiological framework of Western Energetic Herbalism.

While Traditional Chinese Medicine looks at Yin/Yang and Ayurveda looks at Doshas, modern Western herbalism uses this six-tissue-state model to categorize how tissues change when they drift into a state of disease. Instead of treating a disease by its Western medical name, a Western energetic herbalist reads the underlying tissue state — Cold/Depressed versus Hot/Irritated, for example — and applies herbs with the opposite energetic qualities to restore balance.

The Six Tissue States

The six tissue states form three pairs of opposing physiological conditions: Dry/Atrophy against Damp/Stagnation, Cold/Depression against Hot/Irritation, and Wind/Constriction against Damp/Relaxation.

1. Hot / Irritation (Hyperactivity)

Tissue is over-stimulated, hyperactive, and inflamed, with metabolic processes running too fast, leading to rapid cellular breakdown.

  • Signs: Redness, sharp or burning pain, heat radiating from the skin, rapid pulse, sharp dry coughs.
  • Therapeutic strategy: Refrigerants, sedatives, and cooling vulneraries to calm metabolic activity and soothe tissue.
  • Classic herbs: Althaea officinalis (Marshmallow root), Rosa spp. (Rose), Stellaria media (Chickweed).

2. Cold / Depression (Hypoactivity)

Tissue function is sluggish and depressed, with constricted local capillaries causing poor blood flow, low oxygen, and metabolic waste buildup.

  • Signs: Pale or bluish skin, cold hands and feet, dull aching pain, thick white tongue coating, a slow deep pulse.
  • Therapeutic strategy: Stimulants and warmers to increase circulation and spark cellular function.
  • Classic herbs: Zingiber officinale (Ginger), Capsicum annuum (Cayenne pepper), Zanthoxylum spp. (Prickly Ash).

3. Dry / Atrophy (Lack of Moisture)

Tissue lacks proper nutrition and moisture, preventing healthy secretion and leading to withered, brittle, atrophied structure.

  • Signs: Parched cracking skin, dry withered tongue, hard dry stools, emaciation, a thin wiry pulse.
  • Therapeutic strategy: Demulcents and tonics to supply mucilage, draw water into parched tissue, and rebuild structure.
  • Classic herbs: Glycyrrhiza glabra (Licorice root), Ulmus rubra (Slippery Elm), Symphytum officinale (Comfrey).

4. Damp / Stagnation (Fluid Buildup)

Lymph nodes, intercellular spaces, and fluids become bogged down as the body's natural drainage backs up.

  • Signs: Swollen lymph nodes, puffy eyes, fluid retention, a thick greasy tongue coating, a heavy slippery pulse.
  • Therapeutic strategy: Alteratives and lymphatics to move stagnant waste toward the eliminative organs.
  • Classic herbs: Galium aparine (Cleavers), Arctium lappa (Burdock root), Phytolacca americana (Poke root — used only in micro-doses, given its toxicity at higher amounts).

5. Wind / Constriction (Spasm & Tension)

The nervous and muscular systems alternate unpredictably between tension and sudden release, creating shifting patterns of pain, cramping, and systemic tightness.

  • Signs: Muscular twitching, dry cramps, shifting or radiating pain, intense anxiety, asthma attacks, a tight bounding pulse.
  • Therapeutic strategy: Antispasmodics and nervines to relax tight muscle fibers and calm the hyperactive nervous system.
  • Classic herbs: Lobelia inflata (Lobelia), Viburnum opulus (Cramp Bark), Passiflora incarnata (Passionflower).

6. Damp / Relaxation (Tissue Laxity)

Tissue lacks structural tone and elasticity, with weak cellular boundaries allowing moisture to leak freely.

  • Signs: Profuse sweating, chronic diarrhea, prolapsed organs, loose joints, a pale wet tongue, a weak soggy pulse.
  • Therapeutic strategy: Astringents and tannin-rich tonics to knit, tighten, and shrink loose tissue.
  • Classic herbs: Quercus spp. (Oak bark), Hamamelis virginiana (Witch Hazel), Agrimonia eupatoria (Agrimony).

Diagnostic Method: Sensory Evaluation

Matthew Wood's framework relies on a hands-on, intuitive yet empirical assessment, reading the body's immediate physical signs rather than laboratory tests.

  • Tongue Evaluation: A dry tongue signals Atrophy; a red tongue signals Irritation; a pale, wet tongue signals Relaxation; a thick, greasy coating signals Stagnation.
  • Pulse Assessment: Speed (hot vs. cold), width (dry vs. damp), and surface tension (constriction vs. relaxation) of the radial artery.
  • Tissue Appearance & Texture: Whether an injured area or organ zone reads as dry and hot, cool and boggy, or tight and spasmodic.

Case Study: Reading the Same Symptom Differently

This model's real strength is showing how the same conventional complaint — chronic joint pain — can reflect entirely different underlying tissue states, each calling for a different herbal approach. (Persistent joint pain and swelling can have many underlying causes, including autoimmune and infectious conditions that require proper medical evaluation — this example illustrates the energetic reading method, not a substitute for diagnosis.)

Patient A (Hot/Irritation): Joint is red, swollen, and hot to the touch, throbbing constantly. Protocol: cooling anti-inflammatories like Willow bark (Salix spp.) or Chickweed to clear the heat.

Patient B (Damp/Stagnation): Joint is cool but heavily swollen with fluid — puffy, soft, boggy. Protocol: moving lymphatics like Cleavers to clear stagnant fluid from the joint space.

Patient C (Dry/Atrophy): Joint isn't swollen but pops, clicks, and feels stiff, lacking cartilage fluid. Protocol: moistening demulcents like Solomon's Seal or Marshmallow root to restore healthy secretion to the joint capsule.
